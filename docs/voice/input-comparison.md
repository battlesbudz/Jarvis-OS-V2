# Voice input comparison and automatic speaker preference

Voice settings select the recognizer used by real calls and post-interruption corrections. Whisper base.en INT8 is the initial selection for this experiment; Moonshine remains selectable. Selection is saved only after model installation succeeds. Both remain offline after downloads. Whisper is endpoint-only, not native streaming; its final decode cost replaces Moonshine's live partials/speculative generation.

“Compare both on one 8-second recording” installs both recognizers, records once with the same Android noise suppression and bounded gain used in calls, releases the microphone, then decodes identical samples sequentially. No Gemma, tools, TTS or preference training occurs in the test. Stay silent to measure background transcription, or speak a known phrase to compare words and decode time. Results persist in ASR diagnostics. These measurements are offline decode timings, not simulated live-call latency. Raw test audio remains in memory only.

Automatic speaker preference is always part of voice-call capture, not a mode requiring enrollment. It learns embedding centroids from consistent first/last 1.5-second voiced windows in completed turns after separate activations. Each activation contributes at most four seconds of weight; one long recording cannot dominate. Training requires 3–12 seconds of voiced audio and at least three words. A preference becomes usable after three matching activations, at least six seconds of capped weight, and more than twice any competing cluster's weight. After learning, unrelated voices cannot silently replace it. Reset is available in voice settings.

The owner is not known from silence or duration alone. Accidental TV wakeups can contaminate initial learning; these rules reduce that risk, not eliminate it. This is a speaker preference, not speaker authentication or source separation. Overlapping owner/TV speech and short commands may remain uncertain. Thresholds (cluster 0.65, reject below 0.35 on both windows) are provisional and require actual phone recordings. Uncertain/short samples are accepted rather than blocking the owner. Rejected candidates never reach final ASR/Gemma/tool dispatch; capture resets and keeps listening within the original inactivity budget. Keyword stop itself remains responsive before verification; the subsequent correction is checked.

Diagnostics report preference scores, voiced duration, embedding compute time, learning/rejection decisions and actual ASR engine/version. No embeddings are included in diagnostic logs. The quiet-speech fallback now requires sustained evidence (300 ms plus stable multiword text) instead of bypassing VAD frame confirmation on a single probability spike. Ordinary confirmed VAD and quiet continuation retain their paths; blank ASR still permits Gemma audio fallback after speaker checks.

Models: Sherpa 1.13.7, Whisper base.en encoder/decoder INT8 and tokens; WeSpeaker English VoxCeleb ResNet34 LM. Release downloads are size/SHA-256 verified, staged, and installed atomically; the APK does not bundle their weights. Whisper initial download is about 209 MB and the speaker model about 27 MB. Sources: https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/tree/v1.13.7/sherpa-onnx/kotlin-api and https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases.
